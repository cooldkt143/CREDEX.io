    Fusion is the energy source that lights up the stars. Here’s the physics behind my belief that it can power the grid, too.
    Commonwealth Fusion Systems

    Commonwealth Fusion Systems

    2w
    Bill Gates shared this
    Today, CFS laid the physics foundation for our ARC fusion power plant. With five deeply researched papers — validated by independent peer review and now published in the Journal of Plasma Physics — we’ve shown why we’re confident that we’ve nailed the scientific basics of producing copious amounts of fusion power and handling fusion’s biggest challenges. ⚛️ How we’re proving the science Fusion, the power of the stars, is hard to bring to Earth. A power plant must control a superhot, unruly cloud of charged particles called a plasma so those particles fuse together and release energy. We’re proving our approach with our SPARC tokamak, the machine we’re building now in Massachusetts, and these new research papers show how our SPARC approach largely carries over to our ARC power plant. The collective assessment from the papers’ 58 co-authors at CFS and collaborating institutions: This machine will work. No scientific breakthroughs are required to bring this clean, secure, abundant source of energy to the electricity grid. Their research builds on decades of tokamak research, backed up by supercomputer simulations that model the plasma down to the level of individual plasma particles. “Designing a fusion power plant that we truly intend to build soon means that we had to ask the right questions, sharpen our tools, and focus on the top-priority information that SPARC will teach us,” said Alex Creely, Chief Engineer of ARC Conceptual Design at CFS. ⚡ Powering the grid The new papers explain how our tokamak will use extremely strong magnets to manage our plasma, confining it at high enough temperature and density for a long enough time to generate 1.1 gigawatts (GW) of fusion power. We’ll convert that into 400 megawatts (MW) of continuous net electricity for the grid, enough to power about 280,000 average American homes. The papers also discuss how we’ll handle major challenges. That includes handling the heat exhaust that’s a key practical constraint for tokamaks, managing disruptions that can derail power generation if not handled well, and keeping the plasma stable for top performance. Swipe through the carousel below to see some of the details. 🚀 What comes next These results are based on a recent ARC plant design, but already we’ve begun working on improvements. The tools we developed for these papers let us integrate different aspects of fusion physics so we can rapidly try new improvements. The core science will still largely apply. If you want to check our math, the full papers are available online. With this transparency, you don’t have to take our assertions on faith. As Principal Scientist Jon Hillesheim put it: “Fusion progress sometimes has been slow. But now in the physics community, people are looking at us and realizing we’re really pushing fusion forward.” #FusionEnergy #PowerMoves #Physics #Science

    We’re in a golden age of Alzheimer’s research, and I couldn’t be more excited. After decades of setbacks, breakthroughs are on the horizon that could change the trajectory of the disease. ​ Simple blood tests can now diagnose Alzheimer’s, enrollment in clinical trials is accelerating, and the FDA has approved the first two treatments proven to slow the progression of the disease, especially when administered early. ​ Another thing fueling my optimism is the way AI is accelerating innovation. By allowing researchers to process enormous amounts of biological and clinical data in a fraction of the time, the technology could help find meaningful patterns that transform our approach to the disease.​ We’ve seen this kind of transformation before. There was a time, not too long ago, when an HIV diagnosis was a death sentence. But once scientists began to understand how the disease works, innovation accelerated, and outcomes changed dramatically. ​ I’m optimistic that, one day, we’ll be able to talk about Alzheimer’s the same way: https://lnkd.in/eGMf9CeZ

    Alzheimer’s research has historically faced problems with scale: not enough people diagnosed early enough, not enough people in clinical trials, not enough data sharing to learn more.
